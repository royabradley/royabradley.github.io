The Margin Review wanted to know why readers finished some essays and abandoned others of the same length. We watched forty-one people read, and asked them afterward what they remembered.

## How the study ran

Nobody read straight through. Every single reader scanned first — down the headings, past the pull quotes, to the end — and only then went back to the top. The ones who abandoned an essay were the ones whose first scan turned up nothing to land on.

## What the finishers had in common

<div class="not-prose breakout">
  <Features
    title="Findings"
    items={[
      {
        title: 'A visible spine',
        body: 'Essays with descriptive headings were finished at roughly twice the rate of essays with clever ones. Readers used headings as a map, not as jokes.',
      },
      {
        title: 'Somewhere to stop',
        body: 'Readers who put an essay down and came back later almost always resumed at a section break. Essays with no breaks were rarely resumed at all.',
      },
      {
        title: 'One claim per screen',
        body: 'Paragraphs that made two arguments at once were the most common point of abandonment, well ahead of length.',
      },
    ]}
  />
</div>

None of this is a case against long essays. The essays readers finished were not shorter. They were easier to re-enter.

## What we changed, and when

<div class="not-prose breakout">
  <Timeline
    title="Rollout"
    items={[
      {
        date: 'January 2026',
        title: 'Headings rewritten',
        body: 'Every heading in the archive was rewritten to say what its section argues.',
      },
      {
        date: 'March 2026',
        title: 'Section breaks added',
        body: 'Essays over fifteen hundred words got breaks whether the author had planned them or not.',
      },
      {
        date: 'June 2026',
        title: 'Contents restored',
        body: 'A collapsible contents list returned to the top of every long piece.',
      },
    ]}
  />
</div>

Completion on new essays rose from thirty-one to fifty-four percent over the two issues that followed. The archive, where we changed only the headings, moved almost as much.
