This page is the working reference for `<Figure>`. Copy from it. The data in every chart is made up. Each figure numbers itself in page order, and the rules each one follows are stated just before it. The checklist at the end sums them up.

## Images

A raster image goes through `src` and `alt`. The component builds the `srcset` and `sizes`, so the browser downloads the smallest file that fills the column. The caption states what the reader should take from the image; the `alt` describes what is in it.

## Charts in the reading column

Label lines directly, at their ends, instead of using a legend. Give every number a comparison and a unit. Use `text-primary` for the one series the caption is about, and `currentColor` or `text-muted` for everything else.

A column chart is drawn for the narrowest screen. Its `viewBox` is 640 wide and its labels are 17 units, so they read at about 9px on a phone and 15px on a desktop. Leave room at the right edge for the end labels: the `overflow-x-auto` wrapper clips anything outside the `viewBox`.

This chart names itself with `aria-labelledby`, which points at its own caption: pass `id` to `Figure` and the caption gets `<id>-caption`. The other charts on this page use a `<title>` instead. Either is fine; every chart needs one or the other.

A figure doesn't have to be a picture. When the exact values matter more than the shape, a table is the better figure.

## Captions with markup

When a caption needs italics, Greek or Hebrew, or math, put it in a `slot="caption"` element instead of the `caption` prop.

## Numbering and cross-references

Figures number themselves with a CSS counter, so the numbers can't be referenced automatically. Write them by hand, as in "the table in Figure 3 has the values behind Figure 2", and check them whenever you reorder figures.

Theorems keep their own counter, so a page can mix the two. This one reads Theorem 1 after four figures:

## Wide figures

A wide figure leaves the reading column for data that needs horizontal room, usually a long time series. Its caption stays on the reading column, aligned with the body text.

On a phone, a wide chart scrolls sideways instead of shrinking: `min-w-[40rem]` on the `<svg>` keeps its labels at full size, and the figure's own wrapper scrolls while the page doesn't.

## Margin figures

A margin figure holds a small, supporting trend beside the paragraph that discusses it. Label its two ends; there is no room for anything else.

<Figure width="margin" caption="Weekly visits rose steadily all year.">
  <svg role="img" viewBox="0 0 200 60" width="100%" class="block h-auto">
    <title>Weekly visits over a year, rising from about 200 to about 900.</title>
    <polyline class="text-muted" points="30,52 53,48 77,44 100,40 123,30 147,24 170,16" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.5" />
    <circle class="text-primary" cx="170" cy="16" r="3" fill="currentColor" />
    <g class="font-sans" font-size="12" fill="currentColor">
      <text x="26" y="56" text-anchor="end" class="text-muted">200</text>
      <text x="177" y="20" class="text-primary">900</text>
    </g>
  </svg>
</Figure>

From 80rem up, the small figure beside this paragraph sits in the right margin, in the same column as the sidenote this footnote creates.[^1] Below that width it appears in the reading column, capped at 20rem so a small figure stays small, and the footnote moves to the end of the page. That covers phones and most iPads; only a 13-inch iPad Pro in landscape is wide enough for the margin. The figure and the sidenote stack instead of overlapping.

## Checklist before publishing a figure

- The caption is a full sentence that states the message, not a label.
- Lines and points are labelled directly; there is no legend.
- Every number has a comparison and a unit.
- At most one series uses `text-primary`.
- Every chart SVG has `role="img"` and a name: a `<title>`, or `aria-labelledby` pointing at the caption.
- Real data has a `source`.
- Column charts use labels of 16–18 units in a 640-wide `viewBox`; wide charts carry `min-w-[40rem]`.
- Cross-references to figure numbers are still right after any reordering.

[^1]: A sidenote, sharing the margin column with the figure.
