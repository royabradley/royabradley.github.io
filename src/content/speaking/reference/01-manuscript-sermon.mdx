A manuscript is the full text of what was preached, set as long-form reading. This page is one, and it shows each piece a manuscript can use. The frontmatter that produced it is at the end.

## What the page builds for you

Everything above the first heading comes from frontmatter. The metadata line reads the fields in a fixed order: kind, passage, part, form, date, place, occasion. The series box lists the series in `part` order and marks this page. The recording follows it, because in a manuscript the text comes first and the audio accompanies it. Then the contents box, built from the headings in the body once there are three or more.

## Writing the body

Write the sermon as you would for print. Headings mark its movements, and should say what each movement claims, not just what it covers.

Greek and Hebrew go straight into the text: *makarioi*, μακάριοι, or *ʾašrê*, אַשְׁרֵי. Italicize transliterations; leave the original script upright.

Exegetical asides belong in footnotes.[^1] From 80rem up they sit in the margin beside the line that calls them; on phones and most iPads they collect at the end, with links back.

Scripture can be set as a block quotation when the wording itself is the point:

> Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. (Matthew 5:3)
